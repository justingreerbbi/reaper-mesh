Why Reaper Mesh

Communication you can depend on when the network can't.

A deliberately small, stable radio core, messages that tell you exactly how far they got, and new features built into the apps, so your radios rarely need updating.

Works off the grid

Radio to radio, with no towers, Wi-Fi or servers in between. Every radio can relay, so adding radios extends the reach.

Honest delivery

Every message shows queued, transmitted, delivered, failed or unknown. Nothing is marked delivered unless the other radio confirmed it.

Never sends twice

If a link drops mid-send, the app asks the radio what happened instead of guessing or resending.

Private when you need it

Encrypted channels keep message text between key holders, and team networks rotate their keys to keep outsiders off your network.

Your team on the map

Positions, shared points and areas, distance measuring and MGRS, with map areas you have viewed kept for offline use.

Cheap, open hardware

Runs on Heltec and LILYGO boards that cost a few tens of dollars. Firmware, apps, protocol and docs are open source.

See it in action

Everything your team needs, on one screen.

Built for gloves and mounted phones: large touch targets, a night palette that keeps your eyes adjusted, and a layout that works on a vest, dash or handlebar mount.

How it works

Your phone talks to your radio. Radios talk to each other.

  1. Your appPhone or browser
  2. USB or Bluetooth
  3. Your radioLoRa board
  4. LoRa
  5. RelayAny radio in between
  6. LoRa
  7. Your teamTheir radio and app

  1. You write a message

    On an encrypted channel the app encrypts it first, so the radio only ever sees scrambled bytes.

  2. Your radio sends it

    Over LoRa, within an airtime budget so no one floods the channel.

  3. Other radios relay it

    A limited number of hops, to reach people beyond direct range.

  4. Their radio confirms

    The receiving radio acknowledges it, and your app shows it as delivered.

A working prototype

Two radios exchange messages reliably, and encryption and team networks work. Relaying through a third radio, outdoor range and long-running durability are still being field-tested, and the security design has not been independently audited. Don't rely on it for emergencies yet. Supports the US 915 MHz band only; always attach the antenna before transmitting.
